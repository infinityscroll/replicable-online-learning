\title{Near-Square-Root Regret for Adversarially Replicable Experts}
\author{Jitendra Prajapati}
\date{October 22, 2026}
\begin{document}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
We study the full-information experts problem with adversarial replicability: two independent cost sequences from any fixed, time-varying product law must induce identical entire action paths when the learner uses the same random tape. We give a causal algorithm with worst-case expected regret $O_{n,\rho}(\sqrt T\log^{7/2}(2T))$ for every fixed number of experts $n$ and replication parameter $\rho$. A two-expert construction combines a relay with a history-dependent switching threshold, dyadic evidence windows, and a rare compensating prediction rule. A tournament reduction uses modal output paths only in its analysis to preserve the product-law guarantee. The dependence on $n$ and $\rho$ is poor. The result concerns expected regret and does not establish a strict $O(\sqrt T)$ bound, efficient finite-precision implementation, or a guarantee for adaptive input laws.
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction and model}
Ahmadi, Bhandari and Blum~\cite{abb} introduced adversarial replicability
for online learning. In their experts model, an oblivious adversary fixes
an arbitrary sequence of distributions over cost vectors. Two independent
draws from those distributions, processed with the same internal random
tape, must produce the same \emph{entire} sequence of expert choices with
high probability. Their Theorem~4.1 gives an experts regret bound
$\widetilde O(T^{5/6}(\ln n)^{5/6}\rho^{-1/3})$.
We obtain a square-root dependence on the horizon, up to logarithmic
factors, when $n$ and $\rho$ are fixed. Our dependence on those two
parameters is substantially worse. This is a comparison with the stated
theorem, not a claim that its bound is the best currently available.

The construction has three parts. A two-expert rule changes action only
when a public dyadic window supports the change. Its threshold grows with
an input-only measure of profitable interval variation. A second rule,
used on a small probability branch, compensates for a variation-dependent
term in the first rule's expected regret. Finally, a binary tournament
extends the result to finitely many experts. For the tournament's
replication proof, we replace each child's random path by a modal path
\emph{in the analysis only}. This avoids assuming that the actual
adaptive losses seen by the parent are independent across time.

The compensating rule uses Cover's classical backward fair-future
betting construction~\cite[Section~3, equations (3.6)--(3.8)]{cover},
extended from binary to $[-1,1]$ inputs by coordinatewise convexity.
We include the short argument in Section~\ref{sec:donor}; the
fair-future formula and this immediate extension are not claimed as
new contributions.
The new bound retains logarithmic factors. It therefore does not resolve
the literal alternative in Section~6 of~\cite{abb}: a strict
$O(\sqrt T)$ upper bound versus an $\omega(\sqrt T)$ lower bound at fixed
replication probability.

\paragraph{Model and conventions.}
There are $n$ experts and a known horizon $T$. At time $t$, the learner
chooses $I_t\in[n]$, observes $c_t\in[0,1]^n$, and incurs $c_t(I_t)$.
The choice is a measurable function of $c_1,\ldots,c_{t-1}$ and an
input-independent tape $R$. For each deterministic input $c_{1:T}$,
define
\[
 \Reg_A(c_{1:T})=
 \EE_R\sum_{t=1}^T c_t(I_t)-\min_{i\in[n]}\sum_{t=1}^T c_t(i).
\]
The expectation includes every branch of the original tape. Regret is
not truncated at zero and need not be nonnegative on an individual
input. We do not require a seedwise or high-probability regret bound.

For every fixed sequence of distributions $D_1,\ldots,D_T$ on
$[0,1]^n$, let $C,C'$ be independent draws from
$P=\bigotimes_{t=1}^T D_t$, independent of $R$.
The learner is \emph{adversarially $\rho$-replicable} if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:model-replication}
 \PP_{R,C,C'}\{ I_t(C,R)=I_t(C',R)\text{ for all }t\in[T]\}
 \ge 1-\rho.
\end{equation}
There is no identical-distribution assumption, and coordinates of a
single cost vector may be dependent. Independence across times and
oblivious choice of the laws are essential to the stated model.

All logarithms are natural unless the base is displayed. Put
$z_+=\max\{z,0\}$ and $\clip(z)=\max\{-1,\min\{z,1\}\}$.
For two experts write $x_t=c_t(2)-c_t(1)\in[-1,1]$ and let
$A_t=+1$ select expert~1 and $A_t=-1$ select expert~2. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reward-regret}
 S_t=\sum_{i=1}^t x_i,\qquad
 G_T=\sum_{t=1}^T A_tx_t,\qquad
 \Reg_A=\frac{|S_T|-\EE_R G_T}{2}.
\end{equation}
This identity follows by subtracting the common cost
$(c_t(1)+c_t(2))/2$ at each time. It imposes no complementary-cost
restriction on the original input vectors.

\section{Main guarantees and finite parameters}\label{sec:main}
For $T\ge2$ define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:universal-parameters}
 K=2\lceil\log_2(T+1)\rceil+2,\qquad
 J=1+\lfloor\log_2(T-1)\rfloor,\qquad
 \gamma=\sqrt{8\ln(2T)}.
\end{equation}
For an outer replication parameter $\rho\in(0,1)$ set
\begin{align}
 \beta&=\rho/4,& \lambda&=\frac{\beta}{1-\beta},& r&=\rho/2,
 \label{eq:mixture-parameters}\\
 \alpha&=\lambda/120,&
 \epsilon&=\frac{\lambda}{80(K+1)},&
 \eta&=\sqrt{2T\ln(16T/r)}.\label{eq:relay-parameters}
\end{align}
We use the explicit threshold
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H-choice}
 H(T,\rho)=\max\left\{
 \frac1\epsilon,\quad
 \gamma\sqrt{(K+1)T},\quad
 \frac{128J\eta}{\alpha\epsilon^2 r}
 \right\},
\end{equation}
where all quantities on the right have the dependence in
\eqref{eq:universal-parameters}--\eqref{eq:relay-parameters}. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:B-choice}
 B(T,\rho)=10H(T,\rho)+\frac{\rho}{8}(\sqrt T+1).
\end{equation}
For each fixed $\rho$, this is $O_\rho(\sqrt T\log^{7/2}(2T))$:
$J,K=O(\log(2T))$, $\epsilon^{-1}=O_\rho(K+1)$, and
$\eta=O_\rho(\sqrt{T\log(2T)})$.

\begin{theorem}[Two experts]\label{thm:two-expert}
For $T\ge2$ and $\rho\in(0,1)$, the algorithm specified in
Sections~\ref{sec:relay} and~\ref{sec:donor} has
$\Reg_A(c_{1:T})\le B(T,\rho)$ on every deterministic
$[0,1]^2$ cost sequence and satisfies~\eqref{eq:model-replication}.
For $T=1$, a shared uniform expert has exact replication and expected
regret at most $1/2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Fixed finite number of experts]\label{thm:main}
For $n\ge2$, let $N=2^{\lceil\log_2 n\rceil}$. There is a causal
full-information adversarially $\rho$-replicable learner with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:n-bound}
 \sup_{c_{1:T}\in([0,1]^n)^T}\Reg_A(c_{1:T})
 \le (\log_2 N) B(T,\rho/N^2),\qquad T\ge2.
\end{equation}
Consequently its regret is
$O_{n,\rho}(\sqrt T\log^{7/2}(2T))$ for every fixed $n$ and $\rho$.
For $n=1$ the sole expert suffices. For $T=1$, a shared
input-independent expert choice has exact replication and regret at most
one.
\end{theorem}
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:two-expert} concludes in
Section~\ref{sec:donor}. The tournament in
Section~\ref{sec:tournament} proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{An interval-variation functional}\label{sec:functional}
Set $S_0=V_0=0$ and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:V-definition}
 V_t=\max_{\mathcal I}
 \sum_{I\in\mathcal I}
 \left(\left|\sum_{i\in I}x_i\right|-\gamma\sqrt{|I|}\right)_+,
 \qquad Q=(V_T-|S_T|)_+.
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal I$ ranges over collections of pairwise disjoint nonempty
integer intervals in $[1,t]$, including the empty collection. The
maximum is finite. In particular $0\le V_t\le t$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:V-properties}
The functional $V_t$ is nondecreasing in $t$, coordinatewise convex,
and $1$-Lipschitz in each input coordinate. For $0\le s<t\le T$,
\begin{align}
 V_t&\ge V_s+(|S_t-S_s|-\gamma\sqrt{t-s})_+,
 \label{eq:V-append}\\
 V_t&\le |S_t|+Q+\gamma\sqrt T.\label{eq:V-prefix}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Append the interval $(s,t]$ to an optimal collection in $[1,s]$ to
obtain~\eqref{eq:V-append}. Retaining a collection proves monotonicity.
For each fixed collection, a coordinate appears in at most one
summand, whose positive-part absolute-value expression is convex and
$1$-Lipschitz in that coordinate. Maximizing preserves both properties.
Applying~\eqref{eq:V-append} to the suffix $(t,T]$ yields
\[
 V_t\le V_T-|S_T-S_t|+\gamma\sqrt T
 \le |S_T|+Q-|S_T-S_t|+\gamma\sqrt T
 \le |S_t|+Q+\gamma\sqrt T.
\]
The case $t=T$ follows directly from $V_T\le|S_T|+Q$.
We will not assume that $Q$ is convex.
\end{proof}

\section{The supported relay}\label{sec:relay}
For the moment take positive parameters $H,\alpha,\epsilon$.
Draw independent $\theta\sim\operatorname{Unif}[-1,1]$ and
$U\sim\operatorname{Unif}[1,2]$, independent of the input. Put
$d=U\epsilon$ and $h_t=H+\alpha V_t$. Start with $A_1=+1$ if
$\theta\le0$ and $A_1=-1$ otherwise.

After observing $x_t$, a \emph{public window} is $(t-L,t]$, where
$L$ is a power of two dividing $t$. Define flags
\begin{align*}
 P_t^+&=\mathbf 1\{S_t-S_{t-L}\ge dh_t
             \text{ for some public window}\},\\
 P_t^-&=\mathbf 1\{S_t-S_{t-L}\le-dh_t
             \text{ for some public window}\}.
\end{align*}
For $t<T$, update by the following fixed rule:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item If $A_t=-1$, $P_t^+=1$, and $S_t>h_t(\theta+d)$, set
      $A_{t+1}=+1$.
\item If $A_t=+1$, $P_t^-=1$, and $S_t<h_t(\theta-d)$, set
      $A_{t+1}=-1$.
\item Otherwise keep $A_{t+1}=A_t$.
\end{enumerate}
Windows are never restarted or truncated at a switch. Ties use exactly
the comparisons displayed above. For the regret proof only, apply the
same update virtually after time $T$; it changes no played action.

Conditional on $U$ and a deterministic input, there is a scalar endpoint
$a_t\in[-1,1]$ such that the action after observation $t$ is $+1$
precisely for $\theta<a_t$, apart from null ties. Initially $a_0=0$.
If $P_t^+=1$, replace $a$ by
$\max\{a,\clip(S_t/h_t-d)\}$; if $P_t^-=1$, replace it by
$\min\{a,\clip(S_t/h_t+d)\}$. These updates commute when both flags
hold, since their thresholds are ordered. Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalar-reward}
 \EE_\theta[G_T\mid U]=\sum_{t=1}^T a_{t-1}x_t.
\end{equation}
This identity uses the original uniform center, without conditioning on
agreement of two input streams.

\section{The compensating prediction rule and the mixture}\label{sec:donor}
Define on $[-1,1]^T$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:F-definition}
 F(x)=\max\{|S_T(x)|,V_T(x)\}=|S_T(x)|+Q(x).
\end{equation}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:V-properties}, $F$ is coordinatewise convex and
$1$-Lipschitz in each coordinate.

\begin{lemma}[Fair-input value]\label{lem:fair-value}
For independent uniform signs $\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_T$,
$\EE F(\xi)\le\sqrt T+1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Hoeffding's inequality gives, for each nonempty interval $I$,
\[
 \PP\left\{\left|\sum_{i\in I}\xi_i\right|>
                      \gamma\sqrt{|I|}\right\}
 \le2e^{-\gamma^2/2}.
\]
If $V_T>0$ at least one interval has this property. There are at most
$T^2$ intervals, so
$\PP\{V_T>0\}\le2T^2e^{-\gamma^2/2}=1/(8T^2)$.
Since $V_T\le T$, its expectation is at most $1/(8T)\le1$.
Finally $\EE|S_T|\le\sqrt{\EE S_T^2}=\sqrt T$ and
$F\le|S_T|+V_T$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fair-future secant prediction]\label{lem:donor}
There is a causal randomized binary-action rule with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:donor-reward}
 \EE_R G_T\ge |S_T|+Q-\sqrt T-1
\end{equation}
on every deterministic input in $[-1,1]^T$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For a fixed prefix $x_{1:t}$ let
\[
 \Phi_t(x_{1:t})=
 \EE_{\xi_{t+1:T}}F(x_{1:t},\xi_{t+1:T}),
 \qquad \Phi_0=\EE F(\xi).
\]
At round $t$, define the fractional prediction
\[
 p_t=\frac{\Phi_t(x_{1:t-1},+1)-
                \Phi_t(x_{1:t-1},-1)}{2}\in[-1,1].
\]
The range follows from the coordinatewise Lipschitz property.
Coordinatewise convexity gives, for the actual $x_t\in[-1,1]$,
\[
 \Phi_t(x_{1:t})\le
 \frac{1+x_t}{2}\Phi_t(x_{1:t-1},+1)+
 \frac{1-x_t}{2}\Phi_t(x_{1:t-1},-1)
 =\Phi_{t-1}(x_{1:t-1})+p_tx_t.
\]
Telescoping yields
$\sum_t p_tx_t\ge F(x)-\Phi_0$.

The exact conditional expectation need not be computed. At round $t$,
sample a fresh independent Rademacher future $Z^{(t)}_{t+1:T}$, and form
\[
 \widehat p_t=
 \frac{F(x_{1:t-1},+1,Z^{(t)}_{t+1:T})-
       F(x_{1:t-1},-1,Z^{(t)}_{t+1:T})}{2}\in[-1,1].
\]
Use a fresh independent uniform variable to choose $A_t=+1$ with
probability $(1+\widehat p_t)/2$. All these variables can be indexed
by round on the original tape before inputs arrive. On each fixed
deterministic input, $\EE_R A_t=p_t$. Thus
$\EE_R G_T\ge F(x)-\EE F(\xi)$, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:fair-value} proves~\eqref{eq:donor-reward}.
The sampled future is artificial randomness; no actual future cost
is accessed. We assert no replication guarantee for this rule alone.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The complete two-expert algorithm.}
With the parameters of Section~\ref{sec:main}, draw one Bernoulli
branch coin, independently of every other tape coordinate. With
probability $\beta$, use the prediction rule of Lemma~\ref{lem:donor}
for the entire horizon. Otherwise use the supported relay. Two replicas
share this branch coin and all other tape coordinates.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:two-expert}]
The parameter choice has $\lambda\in(0,1)$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:good-reward} and the first two terms
of~\eqref{eq:H-choice} give relay reward at least
$|S_T|-\lambda Q-20H$ in original-tape expectation.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:donor}, the full mixture therefore has
\begin{align*}
 \EE_R G_T
 &\ge(1-\beta)(|S_T|-\lambda Q-20H)
           +\beta(|S_T|+Q-\sqrt T-1)\\
 &=|S_T|-20(1-\beta)H-\beta(\sqrt T+1),
\end{align*}
because $(1-\beta)\lambda=\beta$.
Equation~\eqref{eq:reward-regret} gives
$\Reg_A\le10H+(\beta/2)(\sqrt T+1)=B(T,\rho)$.
No branch or negative-regret contribution has been discarded.

For every fixed product law, Lemma~\ref{lem:relay-replication}
and the third term in~\eqref{eq:H-choice} bound the relay's entire-path
failure by $r$. Charge the compensating branch its full probability
$\beta$, whether or not its two paths happen to agree. Independence of
the branch coin from all inner randomness yields failure at most
\[
 \beta+(1-\beta)r=\rho/4+(1-\rho/4)\rho/2<\rho.
\]
The arbitrary cost-vector reduction was established
in~\eqref{eq:reward-regret}. For $T=1$, a shared uniform expert is
independent of both costs, replicates exactly, and has expected regret
at most $1/2$.
\end{proof}

\section{Computation and scope}\label{sec:scope}
The interval functional admits the dynamic program
\begin{equation}\label{eq:DP}
 V_t=\max\left\{V_{t-1},\max_{0\le s<t}
 \left[V_s+(|S_t-S_s|-\gamma\sqrt{t-s})_+\right]\right\}.
\end{equation}
To verify this identity, take the last interval of an optimal collection.
If it ends before $t$, its value is at most $V_{t-1}$; otherwise its
predecessors lie in the prefix ending at its left endpoint. Conversely
every candidate in~\eqref{eq:DP} is feasible.
Prefix sums and this dynamic program require $O(T^2)$ arithmetic
operations. The relay's public window tests require at most
$O(T\log(2T))$ additional operations. Evaluating $F$ twice for each
fresh artificial future gives a naive $O(T^3)$ arithmetic implementation
of the compensating rule. A tournament with $O(n)$ internal nodes has
the corresponding $O(nT^3)$ arithmetic bound.

These are exact real-arithmetic descriptions. No finite-precision
comparison rule, bit-complexity bound, implementation measurement or
practical speedup follows. The explicit constants and dependence on
$n,\rho$ can make the numerical bound uninformative at moderate
horizons. The asymptotic improvement concerns $T$ with those parameters
fixed. The algorithm knows $T$ and $\rho$; an anytime guarantee is not
claimed.

The rare branch is allowed because regret is an unconditional
expectation over the entire input-independent tape, whereas replication
permits a probability $\rho$ of failure. The proof does not provide a
high-probability or seedwise regret theorem. It also does not extend
the product-law replication guarantee to input laws that adapt to
realized past costs or actions. The logarithmic factors leave the
strict square-root alternative in~\cite{abb} unresolved by this work.
