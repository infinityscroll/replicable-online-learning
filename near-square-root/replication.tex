\section{Whole-path replication of the supported relay}
\label{sec:replication}

The widening statistic $V_t$ and the support windows depend only on the
observed costs, not on the relay's actions. This permits a comparison with
deterministic quantities associated with the input law. In particular, no
part of the following argument conditions on agreement of earlier actions.

\begin{lemma}[Replication of the supported relay]
\label{lem:relay-replication}
Let $T\ge2$, $0<\alpha,\epsilon\le1$, and $0<r<1$. Set
\begin{equation}
 \eta=\sqrt{2T\ln(16T/r)},\qquad
 J=1+\lfloor\log_2(T-1)\rfloor.
 \label{eq:replication-parameters}
\end{equation}
If
\begin{equation}
 H\ge \frac{128J\eta}{\alpha\epsilon^2r},
 \label{eq:replication-width}
\end{equation}
then the supported relay with $h_t=H+\alpha V_t$, shared independent
$\theta\sim\operatorname{Unif}[-1,1]$ and
$U\sim\operatorname{Unif}[1,2]$, and $d=U\epsilon$, is $r$-replicable.
More precisely, for every fixed product law on $[-1,1]^T$, two independent
input streams using the same complete random tape produce identical
played-action paths $(A_1,\ldots,A_T)$ with probability at least $1-r$.
The law may vary arbitrarily across time and is independent of the tape.
The conclusion allows any common tape-measurable initialization of $A_1$,
including the initialization used above.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix such an input law. Write
\[
 \bar S_t=\EE S_t,\qquad D_t=\EE V_t,\qquad
 \bar h_t=H+\alpha D_t.
\]
These are deterministic functions of the fixed law. A relevant monitoring
window is $I=(t-L,t]$ with $1\le t<T$, $L$ a power of two, and $L\mid t$.
Let $\mu_I=\bar S_t-\bar S_{t-L}$. The two streams will be denoted by
superscripts $1,2$ when necessary.

First, for an arbitrary $\eta>0$, there is an event $\mathcal E$ depending
only on the two input streams such that
\begin{equation}
 \PP(\mathcal E^c)\le 8T\exp\!\left(-\frac{\eta^2}{2T}\right),
 \label{eq:replication-concentration}
\end{equation}
and, on $\mathcal E$, for every prefix and both streams,
\begin{equation}
 \abs{S_t-\bar S_t}\le\eta,\qquad
 \abs{V_t-D_t}\le\eta,\qquad
 \abs{h_t-\bar h_t}\le\alpha\eta.
 \label{eq:replication-good-event}
\end{equation}
Indeed, Hoeffding's inequality gives the stated tail bound for $S_t$.
For a fixed interval collection defining $V_t$, a coordinate occurs in at
most one summand, and changing that coordinate changes the summand by at
most the magnitude of the change. Taking a maximum preserves this
coordinatewise $1$-Lipschitz bound. Since each input coordinate lies in
$[-1,1]$, the bounded-differences inequality gives the same tail bound for
$V_t$. A union bound over the two statistics, both streams, and at most
$T$ prefixes proves \eqref{eq:replication-concentration}. Independence
across time is used here; identical distribution is not required.

Appending $I$ to an optimal interval collection in $[1,t-L]$ gives
\[
 V_t\ge V_{t-L}
       +\pos{\abs{S_t-S_{t-L}}-\gamma\sqrt L}.
\]
Taking expectations and using Jensen's inequality yields
\begin{equation}
 D_t-D_{t-L}\ge \abs{\mu_I}-\gamma\sqrt L.
 \label{eq:replication-mean-progress}
\end{equation}
Also $D_t$ and $\bar h_t$ are nondecreasing in $t$.

For the moment assume
\begin{equation}
 \epsilon H\ge4(1+\alpha\epsilon)\eta,
 \qquad
 \epsilon H\ge4\gamma\sqrt T.
 \label{eq:replication-auxiliary-width}
\end{equation}
Call a relevant window a \emph{candidate} if
\begin{equation}
 \abs{\mu_I}\ge\frac{\epsilon\bar h_t}{2}.
 \label{eq:replication-candidate}
\end{equation}
This family is deterministic once the law is fixed. On $\mathcal E$, if
either stream passes either signed support test at $I$ for some
$U\in[1,2]$, then $I$ is a candidate, and the passing sign is
$\operatorname{sign}(\mu_I)$. To see this, let $\sigma\in\{-1,1\}$
be a passing sign. An interval increment differs from its mean by at most
$2\eta$, so
\[
 \sigma\mu_I
 \ge\epsilon\bar h_t-2(1+\alpha\epsilon)\eta
 \ge\frac{\epsilon\bar h_t}{2}>0.
\]
Here the actual passing inequality is
$\sigma(S_t-S_{t-L})\ge U\epsilon h_t$, and we have used a conservative
bound uniform over $U\in[1,2]$. Thus noncandidate windows cannot cause
a switch on $\mathcal E$, and at a candidate the opposite sign cannot
pass.

For a candidate, \eqref{eq:replication-mean-progress} and
\eqref{eq:replication-auxiliary-width} imply
\begin{equation}
 \bar h_t-\bar h_{t-L}
 \ge\frac{\alpha\epsilon}{4}\bar h_t.
 \label{eq:replication-width-growth}
\end{equation}
Set $\kappa=\alpha\epsilon/4\in(0,1/4]$. At a fixed scale $L$, the
public $L$-windows are disjoint. If their candidate endpoint widths in
time order are $b_1,\ldots,b_m$, monotonicity and
\eqref{eq:replication-width-growth} imply
$b_j\ge b_{j-1}/(1-\kappa)$, with $b_0=H$. Consequently
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{\substack{I\text{ candidate}\\\text{at scale }L}}
       \frac1{\bar h_{\operatorname{end}(I)}}
 \le\frac1H\sum_{j\ge1}(1-\kappa)^j
 \le\frac{4}{\alpha\epsilon H}.
 \label{eq:replication-inverse-widths}
\end{equation}
There are $J$ relevant dyadic scales. The endpoint widths and the
candidate family in this inequality depend on neither tape nor realized
input stream.

We next bound the exceptional shared thresholds. Fix any pair of input
streams in $\mathcal E$. Because $\mathcal E$ is input-only, $\theta$
and $U$ retain their independent uniform laws. For a candidate $I$, put
$\sigma=\operatorname{sign}(\mu_I)$ and
$\delta_e=2(1+\alpha\epsilon)\eta$. In either stream, the signed support
expression differs from its deterministic reference
$\sigma\mu_I-U\epsilon\bar h_t$ by at most $\delta_e$. Therefore a
difference between the two support decisions requires
\[
 \abs{\sigma\mu_I-U\epsilon\bar h_t}\le\delta_e.
\]
The possible $U$ values lie in an interval of length at most
$2\delta_e/(\epsilon\bar h_t)$. Since the support of $U$ has length one,
the probability of this event is at most
\begin{equation}
 \frac{4(1+\alpha\epsilon)\eta}{\epsilon\bar h_t}.
 \label{eq:replication-support-strip}
\end{equation}
No additional sign factor is needed: the preceding argument excluded
passage with the opposite sign on $\mathcal E$.

The state tests in the two directions compare
$S_t-h_t(\theta+U\epsilon)$ and $S_t-h_t(\theta-U\epsilon)$ to zero.
Each differs from its corresponding reference with $S_t,h_t$ replaced
by $\bar S_t,\bar h_t$ by at most
\[
 \delta_s=[1+\alpha(1+2\epsilon)]\eta.
\]
For each fixed $U$, disagreement on either test is confined to a
$\theta$-interval of length at most $2\delta_s/\bar h_t$. Uniformity on
$[-1,1]$ and independence from $U$ show that the union for the two
directions has probability at most
\begin{equation}
 \frac{2[1+\alpha(1+2\epsilon)]\eta}{\bar h_t}.
 \label{eq:replication-state-strips}
\end{equation}

Include \eqref{eq:replication-state-strips} once per candidate window;
counting a time more than once only weakens the bound. If no candidate
ends at a time, neither stream can switch then. Otherwise, outside the
support strips all signed support predicates agree, and outside the
state strips both state predicates agree. Starting from the common
$A_1$, induction therefore gives identical played-action paths whenever
none of these strip events occurs. This is a pointwise implication. No
probability estimate has been conditioned on earlier agreement, on the
current action, or on a surviving tape branch.

Applying the union bound and \eqref{eq:replication-inverse-widths} gives,
under \eqref{eq:replication-auxiliary-width},
\begin{align}
 \PP\{(A_t^1)_{t=1}^T\ne(A_t^2)_{t=1}^T\}
 &\le 8T e^{-\eta^2/(2T)}
  +\left[\frac{4(1+\alpha\epsilon)}{\epsilon}
      +2\bigl(1+\alpha(1+2\epsilon)\bigr)\right]
      \frac{4J\eta}{\alpha\epsilon H}\notag\\
 &\le 8T e^{-\eta^2/(2T)}
      +\frac{64J\eta}{\alpha\epsilon^2H}.
 \label{eq:replication-general-bound}
\end{align}
The final inequality uses only $\alpha,\epsilon\le1$.

With \eqref{eq:replication-parameters}, the first term is $r/2$, and
\eqref{eq:replication-width} bounds the second by $r/2$. The auxiliary
conditions also hold: $\gamma\sqrt T\le2\eta$, whereas
\eqref{eq:replication-width} dominates both $8\eta/\epsilon$ and
$4(1+\alpha\epsilon)\eta/\epsilon$. This proves the claimed
unconditional whole-path guarantee.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The use of $V_t$ rather than a surplus that vanishes on monotone paths is
essential to this argument: every possibly active window forces the
growth in \eqref{eq:replication-width-growth}. The independent $U$
smooths the support comparison itself, while $\theta$ smooths the state
comparison. All windows remain public and available after switches.
The proof makes no assertion that close input paths have identical
crossing times without these shared-threshold estimates. For $T=1$, a
common input-independent initialization already replicates exactly.
\end{remark}
